What you're feeling is normal.

The confusion, the fear, the guilt, the relief tangled up with grief - all of it is a natural response to what you've been through. You are not broken. You are healing.

The fog is real

After leaving, many survivors describe feeling unable to think clearly or even name what happened. This fog lifts -at your own pace.

Missing them is normal

The pull to go back isn't weakness. It's a biochemical response to the cycle you survived. On average, survivors leave 7 times before leaving for good.

Healing isn't linear

Some days will feel like progress. Others will feel like starting over. Both are part of the journey, and both are okay.

Phase 2: The Middle

Stuck doesn't mean broken.

You're safe, but the weight of everything is surfacing. Grief for the relationship, anger you couldn't express before, and the question "who am I without this?" are all part of this phase.

Grief without closure

You're mourning someone still alive, a relationship that held both love and violence. Society rarely validates this grief.

Anger is arriving

Anger was unsafe to express during the relationship. Feeling it now is healthy -even when it's frightening.

Identity is rebuilding

Your preferences, opinions, and sense of self were systematically eroded. Rediscovering them takes time and patience.

What might help right now

Trauma-informed yoga for body reconnection → Mindfulness practices for self-exploration → Daily check-ins with the community →
Phase 3: Rebuilding

You're becoming someone new.

Rebuilding trust, setting boundaries, navigating new relationships - this phase is simultaneously liberating and terrifying. Growth and pain coexist here, and that's okay.

Boundaries are new

You may swing between walls and no boundaries at all. Learning the middle ground is a practice, not a destination.

Trust takes time

Learning to distinguish healthy from unhealthy patterns -in others and in yourself -is part of earned secure attachment.

Post-traumatic growth

Deeper empathy, clarity about values, new life directions. Not universal, but possible -and it coexists with ongoing pain.

What might help right now

Wellness programs for ongoing healing → Peer support and mentorship → Weekly community events →
